# Statistical Data Analysis: Baccalaureate Exam Survey in Romania

Academic coursework project developed for Statistics at the Faculty of Cybernetics, Statistics and Economic Informatics, ASE Bucharest.

## Overview
This study provides a descriptive and inferential statistical analysis of the Romanian National Baccalaureate examination based on a survey of 569 respondents segmented across three generations: grandparents, parents, and current youth.

The research investigates discipline choices, weekly study habits, examination stress levels, and the generational rise of private tutoring.

## Key Statistical Findings
* Discipline Distribution: Over 58 percent of candidates concentrated on Mathematics and History as their primary elective subjects.
* Weekly Preparation Effort: Most respondents studied between 4 and 8 hours weekly, with only 4 percent exceeding 16 hours of preparation per week.
* Exam Stress Assessment: The mean stress score was 7.42 out of 10, with a median of 8.00 and a mode of 10, showing a coefficient of variation of 29.78 percent that confirms a homogeneous sample distribution.
* Cross-Generational Private Tutoring: Private tutoring usage increased from 10 percent among grandparents to 38 percent among parents and reached over 69 percent among current students.
* Hypothesis Testing via Chi-Square: The chi-square test yielded a test statistic of 237.59 with a p-value near zero, rejecting the null hypothesis and confirming a strong relationship between generational cohorts and private tutoring dependency.
